# What’s new

Updates you can see in Retro Pokédex. Releases that only changed hosting, search listings, or internal data are left out.

## 0.8.0 — September 27, 2026

- On a Pokémon’s page, every region it appears in has a map tab. Alola says the map is not drawn yet and points to the spawn table.
- The spawn table lists every region at once, not only the map you have selected.
- An evolution line names more of the real requirements. Malamar is while the console is upside down. Escavalier is a trade for Karrablast. Hitmonlee is when Attack is higher than Defense.
- The header uses a new Retro Pokédex logo, and a few screens get small layout tweaks.

## 0.7.9 — September 26, 2026

- My Collection is in the header, between Pokédex and Checklist. It lists every Pokémon you have marked caught or shiny, from every save. Mega Evolutions and Gigantamax stay off the list. The game selector is hidden here.
- Filter by origin region. Alolan Vulpix counts for Alola, not Kanto. Search by name or National Dex number.
- Filter All, Normal, Shiny, Alternate Forms, or Variants. Alolan Vulpix is an alternate form. Blue Floette is a variant.
- An Ultra Ball on a card means a specimen has been uploaded for that Pokémon. An uploaded specimen can show up even if you have not marked it caught.
- Pokédex cards no longer show NORMAL or SHINY in the corner. The catch and shiny buttons are still there.
- The roadmap no longer lists Your collection.

## 0.7.8 — September 26, 2026

- Switch between English and Spanish with EN and ES in the header. The choice is still there the next time you open the site. On a phone, both languages are in the menu.
- The dashboard offers to switch when your connection is in a country that uses the other language. Mexico or Spain can offer Spanish. You can stay in the language you already have.
- Labels follow the language you pick. Sword / Shield is Espada / Escudo. Unova is Teselia. Fire is Fuego. Alolan Vulpix is Vulpix de Alola, and Gigantamax Charizard is Charizard Gigamax.
- On a Pokémon’s page, the name, the Pokédex entry, and the written overview follow that language. A Spanish overview that is not written yet stays in English.
- On a narrow screen, Last caught is a list. Pokédex filters stack, and checklist rows put the catch buttons on their own line. Game tags on a Pokémon’s page use the full width.

## 0.7.7 — September 26, 2026

- The move dialog lists games where that Pokémon can be caught, received, or evolved without a trade. Clawitzer offers X / Y, Ultra Sun / Ultra Moon, Sword / Shield, and Scarlet / Violet. A trade evolution such as Alakazam stays off games where trading is the only way.
- Those choices use the same game tags as the card. A long name such as Omega Ruby / Alpha Sapphire stays on one line.
- When the only way to find a Pokémon in the wild is a rod or a field move, the checklist says so. Magikarp in Red and Blue is fishing with an Old Rod and a Super Rod. Shuckle in Gold and Silver is using Rock Smash. A Pokémon you can also walk into still says Wild Encounter.
- An evolution line states every requirement. Sylveon is level up with high friendship and while knowing a Fairy-type move. Espeon adds during the day, and Umbreon adds at night.

## 0.7.6 — September 25, 2026

- Flower colors such as Blue Floette are Variants. Forms that play differently, such as Eternal Floette, stay under Alternate forms. Each one you can catch is marked on its own.
- The evolution line and each catchable form show a colored Poké Ball once you have marked it caught.
- The Games box lists where the form you are viewing can be found, and how you get it, such as Wild, Gift, Event, or Pokédex. Expansions such as the Teal Mask, the Indigo Disk, the Isle of Armor, and the Crown Tundra are listed on their own.
- On a Pokédex card, click the game tag to move that mark to another save.
- Last caught keeps a variant’s name and sprite. Blue Floette opens that form’s page.
- Search from the dashboard with at least two characters of a name or a National Dex number.

## 0.7.5 — September 25, 2026

- The roadmap lists Switch box screenshots, the region maps still to draw, and Terastallization. Open it from the footer.

## 0.7.4 — September 24, 2026

- Fossil Pokémon have a bronze border, and starters have a gray one. The name and Pokédex number use that same color.
- Pseudo-legendary violet covers the whole evolutionary line.
- A species page shows a rarity label between the Pokédex number and the types. An info button explains that rarity.

## 0.7.3 — September 24, 2026

- Legendary Pokémon have a gold border on Pokédex cards, checklist rows, and the species page.
- Mythical Pokémon have a dark red border in those same places.
- Pseudo-legendaries have a violet border.

## 0.7.2 — September 24, 2026

- The dashboard lists Mega Evolutions, Gigantamax, and Regional Forms. Each box opens that list in the Pokédex.
- Mega Evolutions and Gigantamax show their colored sprite and no catch marks, since they cannot be caught on their own.
- A Mega or Gigantamax page leaves out the Pokédex entry, abilities and natures, wild locations, moves, and your specimens.

## 0.7.1 — September 24, 2026

- A changelog page lists what changed in each release. Open it from the footer.
- Male and Female buttons appear only on a Pokémon’s page.

## 0.7.0 — September 24, 2026

- Alternate forms now have their own details: Mega Evolutions, regional forms, Gigantamax, and similar.
- Switch forms in the Pokédex, the checklist, a Pokémon’s page, and your collection.
- Sprites show the Pokémon’s gender when the male and female look different.

## 0.6.9 — September 23, 2026

## 0.6.3 — September 21, 2026

- Kalos has a region map.
- On the checklist, version exclusives are labeled for the game you have selected (for example, X versus Y).

## 0.6.2 — September 21, 2026

- A checklist for tracking what you have caught and what is still missing.
- Search, filter by status, and switch between the National Pokédex, a regional Pokédex, and the game you are playing.
- Mark a Pokémon caught or shiny from the list.
- Each entry can show a short note on how to get that Pokémon in the selected game.

## 0.6.1 — September 21, 2026

- Region maps use one consistent size for routes, cities, and labels.
- Cities you have not reached yet are easier to see.

## 0.6 — September 21, 2026

- Save the Pokémon you actually own: level, nature, ability, moves, stats, ball, and where you caught it.
- Import one Pokémon file or a batch, then edit or remove those entries.
- Caught and shiny marks are kept per game, separate from the individual Pokémon you import.
- Competitive movesets, plus suggested moves limited to what that Pokémon can learn in the selected game.
- A short overview for each species.
- Reset your password by email.

## 0.5 — September 20, 2026

First release.

- Sign in and keep your own collection.
- Browse the National Pokédex and open any Pokémon for its stats, types, abilities, and moves.
- See type matchups and where a Pokémon appears in the wild.
- Region maps for Kanto, Johto, Hoenn, Sinnoh, and Unova, including a route between locations.
- Track how much of your collection you have caught.
